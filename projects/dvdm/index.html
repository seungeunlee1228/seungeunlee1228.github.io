Distilling Video Diffusion Model to Animate Expressive 3D Humans from a Photo

Seungeun Lee1, Gyeong-Moon Park1,2
KLleon1, Korea University2

TL;DR. End-to-end framework that distills large video diffusion models to generate expressive, synchronized 3D avatars from a single image.

Abstract

Creating lifelike 3D talking avatars from just a single photo has long been a challenge — especially to capture fine facial expressions, hand gestures, and natural audio synchronization. Traditional pipelines first convert audio into pose parameters, then render the result — but this two-step process introduces loss, drift, and unnatural synchronization.

In this paper, we build an end-to-end system that animates a full-body, photorealistic 3D avatar directly from audio, avoiding each modules to be isolated when training. The avatar is represented as a particle-based deformation field of 3D Gaussian primitives, where each particle reacts dynamically to speech features, enabling micro-articulations such as lip closures, cheek inflation, and finger gestures in perfect sync with the voice.

To bring a single photo to life, we distill motion priors from a large-scale video diffusion model in the terms of constructing training dataset and regularizing rendered 3D avatars to score sampling objective function. Trained end-to-end with photometric and temporal objectives, our system jointly learns deformation, appearance, and rendering — achieving state-of-the-art realism, synchronization, and expressivity compared to existing methods of animatable 3D avatars.

Motivation

When animating a 3D avatar with conversational motion from audio, state-ofthe-art pose-driven deformation approach degrades visual quality (including facial expressions), yield less natural motion, and exhibit poor audio–motion synchronization. In contrast, our method directly controls the avatar from the audio signal, yielding substantial improvements in visual quality, motion naturalness, and synchronization.

Method Overview

Model Architecture

Our method builds a personalized 3D Gaussian avatar from a single image using a Hand-Body–Head Transformer that integrates face, body, and hand features. An audio-driven particle deformer then directly maps speech features to Gaussian deformations, generating synchronized full-body motion. By distilling motion priors from a video diffusion model, our system achieves realistic, expressive, and temporally consistent talking avatars.

Data Creation

We build a single-human audio–video paired dataset using an audio-driven video diffusion model. Prompts are generated from a predefined dictionary of human attributes to ensure diverse identities, and these are used with FLUX, Gemini, and ChatGPT text-to-image models to create corresponding reference images. In parallel, conversational scripts are converted to speech via ElevenLabs, and paired with each image. Finally, the Wan2.2 Video Generation Model synthesizes synchronized talking videos, forming a diverse and high-quality dataset for training expressive 3D avatars.

Further Discussion

Limitation: Our system requires high computational cost for 3D avatar personalization — over 40 hours of video data per identity and 1.5–2 hours to generate a 10-second clip with Wan2.2 on an A100 GPU, totaling around ₩30 million on cloud.

Future work: We will explore feed-forward 3DGS models, which allow real-time inference without iterative optimization. By leveraging large-scale web-crawled talking-human videos, we aim to build a more generalized and efficient model for scalable avatar generation.
